Urban PM2.5 Forecasting

A station-aware PM2.5 forecasting pipeline for three monitoring sites in Mashhad. Missing-data repair, temporal features, environmental clustering, optimized regressors, and a local prediction toolbox form one operational workflow.

Abstract

Fine particulate matter with aerodynamic diameter below 2.5 µm (PM2.5) is one of the most important air-quality indicators in large urban areas. Reliable daily prediction of PM2.5 is difficult because urban pollution is affected by traffic, industrial activity, weather, seasonality, and missing observations in monitoring networks. This paper presents a practical and computationally weighted framework for PM2.5 prediction at three monitoring stations in Mashhad, Iran: Torogh (TRG), Sajjad (SJD), and Villa (VLA). The proposed workflow first repairs the data using a PM-specific missing-value strategy based on historical similarity with K-nearest neighbors and station-to-station regression. Then, temporal information is transformed into supervised learning features by adding previous-day and three-day mean particulate variables and the previous-day mean temperature. After standardization and categorical encoding, K-means++ is used to divide the data into environmental regimes. For each station and each cluster, several regressors are trained, including Random Forest, XGBoost, CatBoost, Decision Tree, KNN Regressor, and Linear Regression. A hybrid Golden Eagle Optimizer and Grey Wolf Optimizer is used for hyperparameter tuning, and the best station-level models are integrated through a lightweight meta-learner. The final system is also connected to a local web-based toolbox that receives meteorological and pollution inputs and returns PM2.5 predictions for the three stations. The results show that the cluster-aware optimized framework provides accurate and stable predictions across the stations, with final test R2 values of 0.911, 0.870, and 0.812 for TRG, SJD, and VLA, respectively.

Station-specific evaluation is central to the design because pollution patterns and missing-data behavior can differ across an urban monitoring network. The study combines data repair, short-term temporal features, environmental clustering, and optimized regression into a single operational sequence. Cluster visualizations show how observations are organized in feature space, while predicted-versus-observed curves reveal behavior over the monitoring record. The toolbox translates this modeling sequence into a form that accepts a defined set of environmental inputs and returns station-level estimates. Together, the reported station results and software implementation demonstrate how a heterogeneous urban prediction problem can be organized into local models with a shared processing framework.
