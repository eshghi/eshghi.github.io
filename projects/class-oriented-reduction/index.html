Class-oriented Data Reduction

A density and spatial-ranking method for retaining informative samples while removing redundant observations. The class-aware procedure evaluates data reduction, execution time, and downstream classification quality together.

Abstract

In recent years, significant advancements have been made in the field of data mining and machine learning, leading to the development of advanced algorithms for data processing and analysis. With the increase in data volume and the need for faster and more accurate processing, numerous techniques have been introduced for data reduction and improving the accuracy of predictive models. This paper examines methods of data reduction through sample selection and noise removal, discussing the advantages and disadvantages of each method. The new approach proposed in this paper focuses on selecting important samples and eliminating unnecessary ones, increasing the speed of data reduction while preserving the quality of data learning. This method improves the accuracy of predictive models by examining each class separately and including a representative from other classes. The main challenge this research aims to address is data reduction while maintaining their quality, which is a time-consuming and challenging process. The experimental results show that the proposed approach outperforms CIS, RSI, and RSU methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency. Specifically, in some datasets, execution time has been reduced by up to several thousand times, and the data reduction rate has reached over 98%, without causing any significant decrease in model accuracy.

The method organizes selection around individual classes while retaining representatives of other classes in the local decision context. Density and spatial ranking are used together to distinguish informative samples from redundant observations. This is especially relevant near class boundaries, where reducing the sample count without considering neighboring classes can remove useful discrimination information. The evaluation therefore considers retained data volume, computational time, and downstream classification performance together. Its reported reduction above 98% applies to particular experimental datasets and is interpreted alongside the corresponding predictive results. The contribution is a class-aware selection strategy that makes the trade-off between compactness and classification quality explicit, supported by comparative experiments and visualizations of the data before and after reduction.
