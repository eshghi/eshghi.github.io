LABP Image Retrieval

An image-retrieval method combining multi-layer texture patterns with a joint RGB color representation. The training-free pipeline ranks images by descriptor similarity and is evaluated on Corel collections through retrieval precision and distance-measure comparisons.

Abstract

Artificial intelligence techniques for image pattern recognition and retrieval are pivotal in various applications, particularly in medical imaging. Despite numerous existing methods, the process remains complex and computationally intensive. This study introduces an innovative approach to content-based image retrieval (CBIR) by integrating Local Average Binary Patterns (LABP) and the joint probability distribution of color channels. LABP extends the traditional Local Binary Pattern (LBP) by considering multiple layers of neighboring pixels, enabling a more comprehensive texture representation. Additionally, we propose a novel color feature extraction method based on the discrete joint probability distribution of RGB color channels, providing a robust representation of color information. The effectiveness of the proposed method is validated on the Wang (Corel-1k) and Corel-10k datasets, demonstrating superior precision compared to other state-of-the-art techniques. This work contributes to enhancing CBIR performance by combining these novel features into a unified feature vector, improving efficiency and accuracy, especially in large datasets.

The retrieval pipeline extracts texture and color descriptors from both the query image and each database image. Texture captures the arrangement of neighboring intensities, while the joint RGB representation retains dependencies between channels that separate color histograms can lose. The two feature groups are concatenated into a common representation, and database images are ranked by descriptor distance. The study also compares distance formulations, including an extended Canberra measure, to examine how the choice of similarity function affects retrieval precision. The reported experiments and precision curves support combining complementary texture and color information within a training-free retrieval system. The resulting contribution is a transparent image-search pipeline whose descriptors, distance calculation, and returned rankings can each be inspected separately, providing a practical alternative for collections where training an additional image model is unnecessary.
