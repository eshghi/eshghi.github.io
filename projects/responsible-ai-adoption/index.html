Responsible AI Adoption

A four-layer framework connecting responsible-AI goals, implementation stages, governance, and measured outcomes. It treats adoption as a continuing sociotechnical process whose evidence must be reviewed throughout the system lifecycle.

Abstract

Artificial intelligence can perform impressively in controlled studies and still fail to create safe or useful care. Responsible adoption therefore requires more than choosing an accurate model. It requires a coordinated lifecycle in which clinical need, equity, transparency, privacy, reliability, uncertainty, human agency, accountability, and sustainability are translated into practical decisions. This chapter presents a four-layer framework for that purpose. The first layer defines the goals and requirements of responsibility; the second organizes implementation from use-case definition to retirement; the third assigns governance and evidence at every stage; and the fourth measures outcomes and feeds them back into the system. The framework treats AI as a sociotechnical intervention rather than a stand-alone algorithm. It is intended to help healthcare organizations decide not only whether a model works, but whether it should be used, for whom, under what safeguards, and with what continuing evidence.

The framework makes responsibility operational by assigning decisions, evidence, and ownership across the adoption process. A use case begins with a defined clinical need and a description of who may benefit or be adversely affected. Implementation then connects data preparation, validation, deployment, monitoring, and eventual retirement. Governance specifies who reviews the evidence, how uncertainty is communicated, and how problems lead to corrective action. The outcomes layer closes the loop by assessing clinical usefulness, equity, reliability, and the effect on the people using the system. The chapter's result is a structured adoption framework rather than a model-performance experiment. Its value is the alignment of technical evaluation with institutional responsibilities and observable consequences. By connecting the four layers, it provides a way to trace a high-level principle through implementation choices to the evidence needed for continued use, and to revise the system when its operating context changes.
