Hi-SIB

A continuous optimizer built around adaptive differential search and multiple attraction centers. Benchmarks across three CEC suites examine how social guidance, population reduction, and search history affect performance at low, medium, and larger evaluation budgets.

Abstract

Limited function-evaluation budgets require optimizers to identify promising regions quickly while preserving enough diversity for subsequent refinement. This paper introduces Hi-SIB, a bounded continuous optimizer that augments success-history adaptive differential evolution with individualized multi-center social guidance. At each iteration, several quality-diverse attraction centers are reconstructed from the population. Each agent evaluates these centers using relative objective quality, normalized distance, and bounded crowding, then samples a guide through temperature-controlled Softmax selection. The resulting social displacement is combined with elite aspiration, archive-assisted differential variation, adaptive movement memories, and population-size reduction. Hi-SIB was evaluated on the OPFUNU implementations of the CEC 2020, 2021, and 2022 suites in 10 and 20 dimensions under budgets of 500, 5,000, and 50,000 evaluations. Across the supplied 100-run comparison archives, Hi-SIB obtained mean ranks of 2.333, 2.042, and 2.361, with 34, 36, and 45 first-place conditions, respectively. A separate 30-run CEC 2022 ablation showed that population reduction produced the clearest contribution, whereas several perception terms had small, problem-dependent effects. Overall, Hi-SIB was strongest at low and medium budgets; adaptive differential-evolution variants improved their relative standing at 50,000 evaluations, exposing a specific opportunity for stronger late-stage refinement. The findings support budget-aware efficiency rather than universal dominance across problem classes.

The evaluation separates performance under different computational budgets because early progress and final convergence describe different aspects of an optimizer. The comparison combines independent runs, aggregate rankings, and component-level ablation. Population reduction changes the allocation of later evaluations, while adaptive memories preserve information from successful moves. The resulting evidence identifies where the multi-center guidance is useful and where further refinement is needed. This makes Hi-SIB a study of practical search behavior under limited evaluations, with an implementation and analysis framework that exposes both its strengths and its dependence on the optimization setting.
