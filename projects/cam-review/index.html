A Method-centered CAM Review

A method-centered review of 57 class activation mapping papers. Its taxonomy connects attribution signals, architectural assumptions, and evaluation objectives while keeping faithfulness, localization, and visual appearance conceptually distinct.

Abstract

Class activation mapping (CAM) has become a central family of visual explanation methods in explainable computer vision, yet the literature is now fragmented across CNN post-hoc attribution, weakly supervised localization, transformer token explanations, and foundation-model-era tools. This review provides a method-centered synthesis of 57 CAM-style papers published from 2016 onward. Unlike application-oriented surveys, it focuses on how CAM maps are generated, what evidence units they use, what architectural assumptions they require, and how their explanations should be evaluated. The review contributes: (i) a reproducible, source-counted search and inclusion protocol; (ii) a taxonomy built on attribution signal, architectural dependence, and evaluation objective; (iii) a problem-response ordering of methods within each group, so that later methods are interpreted as answers to earlier limitations; (iv) a protocol-aware comparison that avoids mixing incompatible metrics; and (v) a discussion of open challenges in faithfulness, resolution, causal validity, prompt sensitivity, and computational cost. The main conclusion is that CAMs should not be treated as interchangeable heatmaps: each method answers a specific explanatory question and should be selected according to the target model, evidence source, and evaluation goal.

The synthesis follows the development of explanation mechanisms from their original architectural assumptions to later methods that alter the attribution signal, spatial resolution, or model compatibility. Organizing papers by the problem each method addresses makes relationships between contributions easier to identify. The comparison also distinguishes evidence about class discrimination from evidence about faithful model behavior or object localization. A method can perform differently on these objectives, so the review connects every comparison to the question and protocol that produced it. The resulting taxonomy and visual summaries provide a practical guide for choosing explanation methods and for designing evaluations that match the intended use of a heatmap. They also expose recurring gaps where visually convincing explanations have limited evidence of causal or predictive relevance.
