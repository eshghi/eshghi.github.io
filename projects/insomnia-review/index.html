Insomnia & Data Analytics

A review of student insomnia research organized by population, location, study period, associated factors, and analytical tools. The synthesis identifies recurring determinants and traces the growing use of machine learning in recent studies.

Abstract

Insomnia is recognized as one of the major sleep disorders, with a growing prevalence rate worldwide, exerting various effects on individuals’ physical and mental health. This disorder is characterized by difficulties in falling asleep, restless sleep, and staying awake for extended periods during the night. Insomnia among young adults, particularly students, is of special concern. Conducting detailed and scientific investigations can help develop effective strategies to improve students' sleep quality. In this paper, we analyzed previous studies from multiple perspectives to draw a comprehensive conclusion. These researches were categorized according to various aspects, including the target population, geographical location, and study year. In addition, the factors influencing insomnia, as reported in each study, were extracted. Furthermore, the tools used for data collection and analysis in these studies were reviewed. These tools included questionnaires and methods of data analysis. The role of data analysis methods is crucial, and thus, the evolution of these tools has been examined in this research. In conclusion, the five most significant factors contributing to insomnia in students include anxiety, depression, stress, sleep hygiene, and Internet addiction. The results also show a clear increase in the use of machine learning tools in data analysis across recent years.

The review links the substantive sleep-health questions to the analytical tools used to investigate them. Organizing studies by population, place, and year makes differences in study context visible, while grouping the reported factors reveals recurring psychological, behavioral, and lifestyle associations. The assessment of questionnaires and analytical methods helps explain why findings may differ across studies that nominally address the same condition. Visual summaries describe the distribution of methods, the development of data-driven analysis over time, and the principal groups of associated factors. The contribution is a combined thematic and methodological account of student insomnia research. It identifies repeatedly studied determinants and traces the move from conventional statistical analysis toward machine learning, providing a basis for more carefully designed datasets and future comparative investigations.
