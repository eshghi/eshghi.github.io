Cluster-specific Runoff Prediction

A hybrid runoff-prediction framework that partitions hydrological observations before fitting cluster-specific regressors. Feature relationships, cluster selection, and prediction comparisons show how local modeling responds to heterogeneous runoff conditions.

Abstract

Accurate runoff prediction plays a crucial role in water resource management, flood control, and hydropower generation. This study proposes a novel hybrid regression approach for runoff prediction by integrating clustering techniques as a preprocessing phase with regression algorithms. Initially, the dataset is divided into distinct clusters to capture underlying patterns in the runoff data. Subsequently, a regressor model is trained on each cluster to enhance predictive performance. The proposed methodology is evaluated using real-world hydrological datasets, and its effectiveness is compared against baseline models. Experimental results demonstrate that clustering-based modeling improves prediction quality, as indicated by key performance metrics such as RMSE and R2. The findings suggest that the hybrid regressor method can significantly enhance the reliability of runoff predictions, offering valuable insights for hydrological forecasting and water management applications.

The objective is to account for the fact that similar hydrological conditions can follow different local relationships from those represented by one global regression function. Feature engineering prepares the predictors before clustering, and the clustering stage groups observations with related characteristics. Separate regressors then learn the response within each group. The experimental analysis includes relationships among the input features and comparisons across candidate cluster counts, linking preprocessing choices to the final prediction task. Performance is assessed through error-based measures and explained variance, while the baseline comparison isolates the value of introducing local models. The reported findings support the use of cluster-specific learning for heterogeneous runoff data. They also motivate evaluation on additional rivers and climatic conditions, since the usefulness of a particular partition depends on the distribution of the hydrological observations. The resulting framework provides a structured route from feature preparation to local prediction and comparative assessment.
