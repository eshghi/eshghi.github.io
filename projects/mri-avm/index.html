MRI-based AVM Classification

A multi-view pipeline for classifying cerebral AVM from standard T2 and FLAIR MRI. The research connects image preparation, convolutional and transformer features, and patient-level voting within an inspectable clinical-imaging workflow.

Abstract

Detecting cerebral arteriovenous malformation from standard brain MRI is a challenging image-classification problem because vascular abnormalities may be subtle in routinely acquired sequences. This research investigates whether complementary T2-weighted and FLAIR views can support a patient-level decision about the presence of AVM. The objective is to use information in standard MRI for the initial classification while vascular imaging and specialist assessment support the reference labels.

Images were acquired and assessed in a clinical setting, and the model inputs are drawn from the routinely available MRI sequences. Preprocessing selects informative slices, normalizes image intensity, removes uninformative margins, and standardizes image dimensions. Several views of each case are retained so the classifier can use complementary spatial and sequence information rather than relying on a single image. The implementation explores convolutional and vision-transformer representations, followed by classification and aggregation of predictions. This design permits alternative feature extractors to be studied within a consistent preparation and voting workflow.

The resulting pipeline connects image preparation, feature extraction, and voting within a patient-level architecture. Its figures show how individual views become model representations and how their outputs are combined into the final decision. Making that relationship explicit supports inspection of the path between image-level evidence and case-level classification. It also provides a common structure for comparing feature extractors, sequence combinations, and aggregation choices without changing the overall evaluation logic. The contribution is a documented multi-view research pipeline for studying AVM classification from routinely available MRI. The saved framework and image panels establish the implemented processing stages and create a basis for subsequent analysis of model behavior, diagnostic discrimination, and the consistency of decisions across complementary views. The portfolio focuses on that architecture and its inspectable visual evidence without attaching unsupported performance claims.
