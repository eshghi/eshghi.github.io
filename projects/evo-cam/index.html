Evo-CAM

Evo-CAM searches for image regions that explain a classifier’s decision. Its evolutionary objective combines confidence, insertion and deletion, and spatial coherence. Experiments across convolutional networks and a vision transformer examine explanation faithfulness and localization on ImageNet and MS COCO.

Abstract

Visual explanations are needed to understand the decisions of deep image models. However, many class activation mapping methods form a heatmap through fixed linear combinations of feature maps. Their explanations may therefore be noisy, coarse, or weakly linked to the metrics used for evaluation. In this paper, Evo-CAM is proposed as a gradient-free evolutionary framework. Heatmap generation is formulated as a spatial optimization problem. Candidate maps are evaluated by model confidence, Insertion and Deletion scores, spatial continuity, and a dynamic area constraint. Spatial crossover and semantic mutation are then used to improve the population over several generations. The method is evaluated on ImageNet with VGG-16, ResNet-50, and Vision Transformer models, and on MS COCO for both faithfulness and localization. Ins-Del scores of 0.61, 0.68, and 0.64 are obtained on the three ImageNet models, respectively. On MS COCO, an Insertion score of 0.5403, a Deletion score of 0.0693, and a Pointing Game score of 76.7% are achieved. Ablation, repeatability, and parameter-randomization tests are also reported. The results show that high-resolution and stable explanations can be produced across different architectures without changing or retraining the model.

The optimization objective connects visual appearance with the response of the classifier. Insertion measures how predictions recover as informative regions are revealed, while deletion measures how confidence changes when those regions are removed. The spatial terms encourage connected explanations and control how much of the image is selected. This combination makes the explanation procedure explicit and allows the contribution of each objective component to be examined. Qualitative comparisons show the resulting maps alongside established CAM methods, and the cross-architecture experiments examine whether the same search principle transfers between convolutional and transformer representations. The contribution is an explanation-generation framework that treats faithfulness, localization, and spatial coherence as jointly relevant properties of a visual explanation.
